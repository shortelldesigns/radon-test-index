What the Vermont Department of Health says

  • One in seven Vermont homes has elevated levels of radon. Neighboring homes can have very different levels, so test your own home.
  • Best test: a long-term radon-in-air kit for 3 to 12 months, ideally including a heating season. The Health Department Lab sells long-, medium- and short-term kits; order online or call 802-338-4736 or 800-660-9997 (toll-free in Vermont).
  • Levels: Vermont outdoor air averages 0.4 pCi/L and homes about 2.5 pCi/L. The action level is 4.0 pCi/L; the Health Department and EPA also encourage lowering levels below 2.0 pCi/L.
  • Fixes: active soil depressurization (vent pipe and fan) is the most common system; HRV/ERV ventilation suits moderate levels or supplements ASD. Retest after installation.
  • Retesting: if levels were low, retest every five years or after major changes to the house; with a mitigation system, test every two years.
  • Real estate: Vermont law does not require a radon test in a real estate transaction. For quick tests, EPA recommends two short-term devices side by side.
  • Questions: Vermont Radon Program 802-863-7220 or 800-439-8550 (toll-free in Vermont), radon@vermont.gov (as printed on the Health Department radon page).
  • Radon can also be in well water; this index page covers indoor air only.
