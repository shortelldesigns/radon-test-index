This page does not invent company names, phones, licenses, or addresses. It does not scrape NRPP, NRSB, certifiedradonpros, or other third-party directories into a fake “state roster.”

Prefer NRPP- or NRSB-certified measurement and mitigation professionals, then re-check the credential on the national site before hiring.

What homeowners can do

  • Radon is an invisible, odorless radioactive gas from soil and rock. You cannot see or smell it — testing indoor air is the only way to know a building’s level. This page covers indoor air, not well water.
  • EPA’s action level remains 4 pCi/L. Mitigate if confirmed indoor-air results are at or above that level.
  • Test kits are available at hardware stores and online. MDH also notes you can find kits and measurement/mitigation professionals through the national programs linked from its radon page.
  • Prefer NRPP- or NRSB-certified measurement and mitigation pros; re-check the credential before hiring.
  • MDE publishes an interactive radon map of Maryland for context only — it is not a substitute for testing your own home.
  • Questions: Environmental Health Hotline / Nancy Servatius 866-703-3266 (listed on the MDE Radon Safety page; MDH also references the same 1-866-703-3266 hotline on its radon page).
  • Optional secondary national lookups: NRPP Find a Pro or NRSB Find a Pro.
