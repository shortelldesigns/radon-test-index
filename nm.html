New Mexico Environment Department · Radiation Control Bureau · Indoor Radon Outreach

New Mexico radon (air)

No state company roster to republish. Updated / retrieved 2026-11-09 (US/Pacific).

Find a certified New Mexico radon pro (NRSB directory)

If you are not sure you need a pro yet, see when a DIY radon test kit is enough or how to test a home for radon.

New Mexico does not certify or license radon testers or mitigators, and NMED publishes no public company list to republish. Its Indoor Radon Outreach page says “There is not currently a certification program within the state,” and its FAQ says the State Radon Program “does not train, test, certify, license radon service providers” and “does not advise, mitigate, recommend mitigators, or promote any company.” This page does not invent company names, phones, licenses, or addresses to fill that gap.

Who to hire: NMED points homeowners to the National Radon Safety Board (NRSB) Professional Directory, and its FAQ says a mitigator should be credentialed through one of the two nationally recognized programs: NRPP or NRSB.

What NMED says New Mexico homeowners can do

  • Free test kits: NMED seasonally hands out free radon test kits through a U.S. EPA State Indoor Radon Grant, first come, first served, while supplies last. Request one at freeradon.testkit@env.nm.gov. NMED says testing is best in winter, when doors and windows are usually shut.
  • Test tips: run the short-term test for 3 to 7 days, then mail the sealed kit back to the lab right away (postage is prepaid).
  • Statewide risk: NMED says high radon is common across New Mexico because of its geology, especially in northern mountain and high-altitude areas, and levels needing mitigation have been found in homes in every county. Only a test tells you your home’s level.
  • When to fix: NMED repeats EPA guidance to act at 4 pCi/L or higher and to consider fixing between 2 and 4 pCi/L; it says the most effective fix is hiring a certified radon mitigation contractor.
  • Questions: email radon.questions@env.nm.gov, call the Radiation Control Bureau at (505) 476-8600, or use the national lines 1-800-SOS-RADON (1-800-767-7236) and 1-800-55RADON (1-800-557-2366), all as listed by NMED.
