Wisconsin Department of Health Services

Wisconsin radon (air)

No state license roster to republish. Updated 2026-09-05 (US/Pacific).

If you are not sure you need a pro yet, see when a DIY radon test kit is enough or how to test a home for radon.

Wisconsin does not require radon measurement or mitigation contractors to be state licensed or nationally certified and does not publish a public statewide list of those businesses. This page does not invent company names, phones, licenses, or addresses to fill that gap.

DHS recommends finding a radon professional through an EPA-recognized credentialing body: NRPP or NRSB (the same organizations DHS names on its “How to Find a Contractor” page). Filter by measurement vs. mitigation, then re-check the credential before hiring.

What DHS says homeowners can do

  • About 1 out of 10 homes in Wisconsin has high radon levels. You cannot see or smell radon — testing indoor air is the only way to know a building’s level.
  • EPA’s long-term action level remains 4 pCi/L. DHS recommends every home and building be tested.
  • Test kits are widely available; many regional Radon Information Centers provide kits free or at reduced cost. Start from the DHS radon hub and the testing guidance linked there.
  • DHS publishes an interactive map of past test results for context only — it is not a substitute for testing your own home.
  • Questions or kit help: call 1-888-LOW-RADON (1-888-569-7236) or contact your regional Radon Information Center (linked from the DHS radon pages).
