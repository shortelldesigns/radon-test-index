Idaho Department of Health and Welfare

Idaho radon (air)

No state license roster to republish. Updated / retrieved 2026-10-02 (US/Pacific).

If you are not sure you need a pro yet, see when a DIY radon test kit is enough or how to test a home for radon.

Idaho DHW does not publish a public statewide list of radon measurement or mitigation businesses and does not offer a state radon license roster on its site. This page does not invent company names, phones, licenses, or addresses to fill that gap.

DHW’s “Find a Certified Radon Professional” and mitigation sections point homeowners to nationally certified providers: NRPP or NRSB Find a Professional (the same links DHW publishes). Filter by measurement vs. mitigation, then re-check the credential before hiring.

What DHW says homeowners can do

  • Radon is a gas you cannot see, smell, or taste. It comes from uranium breaking down in soil and rock and can enter a home through cracks in floors, walls, or around pipes.
  • DHW states that 2 out of 5 Idaho homes have elevated radon levels. Levels vary by location — the only way to know is to test.
  • EPA’s guideline remains 4.0 pCi/L. DHW advises mitigating when levels are higher than that guideline.
  • Test the lowest level of the home where people spend time. Short-term tests (about 3–7 days; DHW notes winter is best) or long-term tests (up to a year) can measure radon.
  • Kit options DHW lists: discounted kits via Air Chek, hardware/home-improvement stores, and a free test for Idaho residents (one every two years) when stock is available. DHW’s free-test offer was marked out of stock when this page was retrieved.
  • Idaho’s Radon Library Lending Program (RLLP) lets residents check out a free radon test kit from participating public libraries statewide (DHW publishes the participating-library table on its radon hub).
  • If a test is at or above 4.0 pCi/L, DHW says fix the home — DIY systems or a certified mitigation professional. Consider fixing between 2.0 and 3.9 pCi/L as well. Retest after changes.
  • For new construction, DHW points to International Residential Code Appendix F guidance; those radon-control methods are not required statewide unless a city or county adopts them.
  • Questions: Idaho DHW Environmental Health Program — radon@dhw.idaho.gov or 800-445-8647.
